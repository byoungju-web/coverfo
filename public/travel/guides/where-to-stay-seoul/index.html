Where to Stay in Seoul: A Neighborhood Guide for First-Time Visitors

Seoul's subway makes most areas workable, but your base still shapes your trip. Here is how Myeongdong, Jongno, Seoul Station, Hongdae, Gangnam, Seongsu and Itaewon compare, and how to pick the right one for your plans.

By coverfo · Last checked October 31, 2026 · 8 min read

Seoul is a big city, but its subway is fast and cheap, so almost any neighborhood can work as a base. What changes from one area to the next is the feel of your evenings, how easily you get to and from the airport, and how much walking you do each day. This guide covers the areas first-time visitors pick most often, what each is good and less good at, and how to choose based on your plans rather than on a hotel photo.

How to choose a base in Seoul

Before you compare neighborhoods, decide what matters most for your trip. Three questions settle most cases:

  • How are you arriving? Most international flights land at Incheon Airport, which is linked to central Seoul by the AREX airport railroad. Staying near an AREX station saves you a long first and last day.
  • What do you want to see? Palaces, hanok streets and traditional markets sit in the old center north of the Han River. Big malls, offices and polished dining are mostly south of the river around Gangnam.
  • What do you want from your evenings? Some areas stay loud until late; others go quiet after dinner. Families and light sleepers usually prefer the second kind.

A simple rule: if this is your first trip and you mainly want sights, stay north of the river and within a short walk of a subway station. Walking distance to the station matters more than the exact neighborhood name.

Myeongdong and the old city center

Myeongdong is the default answer for a reason. It is one of Seoul's main shopping districts, packed with cosmetics stores, fashion shops and street food stalls, and it is served by Myeongdong Station on Subway Line 4. From here, the palaces, Namsan and N Seoul Tower, Namdaemun and Dongdaemun are all a short subway ride or taxi away. There is a wide range of hotels, from business chains to compact budget rooms, so it is usually easy to find something that fits.

The downside is the crowds. The main shopping streets are busy from late morning until night, and the area can feel built around visitors rather than locals. If you want the convenience without the noise, look at hotels a few blocks away toward City Hall, Euljiro or Chungmuro. You keep the central location and get quieter nights.

Jongno, Insadong and Bukchon

If history is the main reason for your trip, this is the area to look at. Gyeongbokgung and Changdeokgung palaces, Insadong's craft and tea shops, the small alleys of Ikseon-dong and the hanok houses of Bukchon are all close together. Staying here means you can reach the palaces when they open, before tour groups arrive.

You will also find traditional hanok guesthouses here. Keep in mind that part of Bukchon is a residential area where the district restricts tourist visits in the evening and early morning. Overnight guests in the area are exempt from those visiting-hour rules, but the neighbors are real residents, so keep noise down. Evenings in Jongno are calmer than in Myeongdong or Hongdae, which suits families and anyone who wants an early start.

Tip: Hanok rooms can be small, with shared bathrooms or floor bedding. Read the room description carefully before you book if you need a regular bed or a private bathroom.

Seoul Station and Hongdae: easy airport access

Both of these areas sit on the AREX line from Incheon Airport, which makes arrival and departure days much simpler.

Seoul Station is the terminus of the AREX express train. According to Visit Seoul, the express runs non-stop from the airport in about 40 minutes and costs around 9,500 won each way, while the cheaper all-stop train takes a little under an hour (fares as of October 2026). Seoul Station is also where KTX high-speed trains leave for Busan and other cities, so it works well if you plan day trips. See our Seoul to Busan KTX guide if that is on your list. The area itself is a busy transport hub rather than a place to linger, but Namdaemun Market and Myeongdong are close.

Hongdae, around Hongik University, is known for its youthful atmosphere: street performers, independent shops, cafes, bars and clubs. Hongik University Station is on the AREX all-stop line, so you can ride straight from the airport with your bags. The neighboring areas of Yeonnam-dong and Mangwon are quieter and good for cafes and casual food. The catch is noise: Hongdae is lively late into the night, especially on Fridays and Saturdays, and it is a bit west of the palace district, so plan on 20 to 30 minutes by subway to the old center.

Gangnam, Seongsu and Itaewon

Gangnam, south of the Han River, is Seoul's modern business and shopping district. It makes sense if you are in town for work, a medical appointment, a concert at a southern venue, or if you simply prefer wide streets, big malls and newer hotels. The trade-off is distance: the palaces and old markets take noticeably longer to reach. One practical detail: the Seoul Climate Card unlimited transit pass does not cover the Sinbundang Line, which runs through parts of Gangnam, so check which lines you will actually use before buying a pass.

Seongsu is a former factory and shoemaking district that now holds cafes in converted warehouses, concept stores, pop-ups and galleries. It is on Subway Line 2 and close to Seoul Forest. Hotel choice is more limited than in the center, and it is better suited to repeat visitors who already know the main sights.

Itaewon has the widest mix of international restaurants in the city and an active bar scene. It is central and on its own subway line, but the streets are steep and it can be loud at night.

Practical booking tips

  • Check the walk to the station. Map the route from the hotel entrance to the nearest subway exit. Seoul's hills and long underground passages can turn "near the station" into a ten-minute walk.
  • Look at room size. Many central Seoul hotels have compact rooms. If you travel with large suitcases, check the listed floor area.
  • Know your check-in time. Long-haul flights often land early in the morning. Ask whether the hotel will hold your luggage until check-in.
  • Book early for peak weeks. Spring cherry blossom season, autumn foliage, and major Korean holidays such as Chuseok and Lunar New Year fill up fast.
  • Use a map app that works in Korea. Naver Map and Kakao Map give more accurate walking and transit directions in Korea than many global apps. Our guide to Korea travel apps explains which ones to install.
Tip: For getting around once you arrive, Seoul sells a short-term Climate Card for tourists that gives unlimited subway and bus rides in Seoul for one to seven days. Prices and coverage change, so check the Visit Korea transportation card page before you buy.

Our quick recommendations

If you still cannot decide, match your trip type to an area:

  1. First trip, mainly sightseeing: Myeongdong or Jongno.
  2. Short stay with early flights: Seoul Station.
  3. Nightlife and a younger vibe: Hongdae, or Yeonnam-dong for something calmer.
  4. Business or shopping malls: Gangnam.
  5. Second visit, cafes and design: Seongsu.
  6. Traditional experience: a hanok guesthouse in Bukchon or around Insadong.

FAQ

What is the best area to stay in Seoul for first-time visitors?

Myeongdong and Jongno are the easiest choices for a first trip. Both are central, well connected by subway and close to the palaces, markets and Namsan.

Which area is most convenient for Incheon Airport?

Seoul Station is the end point of the AREX express train from Incheon Airport, which takes about 40 minutes. Hongdae is also on the AREX all-stop line.

Is Gangnam a good base for sightseeing?

Gangnam is modern and well connected, but it is south of the Han River and farther from the palaces and traditional markets. It suits business trips and shopping more than a first sightseeing trip.

Can I stay in a hanok in Bukchon?

Yes, there are hanok guesthouses in Bukchon and around Insadong. Part of Bukchon restricts tourist visits in the evening and early morning, but overnight guests in the area are exempt; rooms are often small, so check the facilities before booking.

Is Hongdae too noisy to stay in?

The main streets are lively late at night, especially on weekends. If you want the area without the noise, look at quieter side streets or nearby Yeonnam-dong.
