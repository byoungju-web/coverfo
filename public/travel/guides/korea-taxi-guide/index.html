How to Take a Taxi in Korea as a Foreigner

Korean taxis are metered, widely available and reasonably priced, but language and app sign-ups can trip up first-time visitors. Here is how to hail or book a cab, what the meter should show, and how to avoid being overcharged.

By coverfo · Last checked October 31, 2026 · 7 min read

Taxis in Korea are plentiful, metered and, by the standards of most big cities, inexpensive. For a first-time visitor they solve a lot of problems: a late flight, a hilltop guesthouse, a dinner reservation across the river, or simply sore feet after a day of palaces and markets. The main hurdles are language and knowing which car you are getting into. This guide covers how to find a cab, which apps work for visitors, what the meter should read, and how to protect yourself from the occasional overcharge. Fares quoted here are for Seoul as of October 2026; other cities set their own rates.

Hailing a taxi on the street

You can still flag a cab from the curb in any Korean city. Look at the small illuminated sign on the windshield or dashboard. A red light showing 빈차 (bincha) means the taxi is free. A green 예약 (yeyak) sign means it has been booked through an app, even if the back seat looks empty, so the driver will not stop for you.

Official taxi stands sit outside train stations, bus terminals, big hotels and airports. Join the queue rather than walking up the line of cars. On busy nights in nightlife areas, drivers often prefer app requests, so a street hail can take a while; this is when an app is worth having.

Before you get in, have your destination ready in Korean. A screenshot of the address or the place name from a Korean map app is far easier for a driver to read than an English name or a hotel booking confirmation.

Tipping is not expected; you pay the meter plus any tolls. If you have large luggage, ask the driver to open the trunk; most will help.

Taxi apps that work for visitors

Most Koreans book cabs by phone, and the apps remove the two biggest pain points for foreigners: explaining where you want to go and haggling over the fare. You set the destination in the app, the driver sees it on a screen, and the route is tracked.

What a Seoul taxi costs

Seoul's regular taxis run on a meter with a fixed base fare plus distance and time charges. The time charge kicks in when the car is crawling in traffic. The table below shows the published rates as of October 2026.

Those late-night figures already include the night surcharge, which works out to roughly 20% in the shoulder hours and 40% in the middle of the night. Deluxe taxis, called 모범 (mobeom) and usually painted black, start at about 7,000 won for the first 3 km and add a 20% surcharge between 10 p.m. and 4 a.m. They are roomier and the drivers tend to be more experienced, but the meter climbs faster.

Leaving the city can add more. A 20% out-of-area surcharge applies on eligible trips beyond Seoul's service boundary, and late-night plus out-of-area charges together can add up to around 60%. Tolls are paid on top of the meter. One useful exception: Seoul taxis travelling between Seoul and Incheon Airport do not charge the out-of-area surcharge, though you still pay the expressway tolls.

App bookings may differ from the street meter. Premium categories such as Blue, Venti and Black have their own pricing, and K.ride adds its service fee. If you book with automatic payment, the fare is charged in the app; do not pay the driver again at the end.

Paying for the ride

Street-hailed taxis take cash, credit cards and transit cards such as T-money. The card reader is usually mounted between the front seats or near the passenger headrest. Hand the card to the driver or tap it yourself when the meter stops, and ask for a receipt (yeongsujeung).

Since December 2025, Seoul taxi receipts show English labels for the final fare, boarding and arrival times, and whether night or out-of-area surcharges were added. That makes it much easier to check what you were charged. The city also asked taxi apps to show the expected fare and expected tolls as separate lines for foreign users, so you can compare the estimate with the final bill.

Card payment is standard in Seoul cabs, but carrying a little cash in won is a sensible backup. If a card terminal is "broken" and the driver insists on a round cash figure, that is a warning sign.

Getting a taxi from the airport

At Incheon International Airport, taxis wait outside arrivals at numbered stands, separated by type and destination. Regular taxis for Seoul, Gyeonggi and Incheon each have their own lines, with deluxe and jumbo vans in a separate queue. Signs at the curb show where each one stops, and the stand numbers can change, so follow the overhead signs rather than relying on a number you read online.

Incheon Airport also has stands for the International Taxi and Smart Taxi services. These are reserved for foreign passengers, have drivers who speak other languages, and charge fixed fares by zone for trips between the airport and Seoul. They cost more than a regular cab but take the guesswork out of a long first ride. You can book ahead or ask at the airport's information desk.

The airport itself warns that unlicensed vans posing as taxis sometimes approach passengers inside the terminal and quote inflated prices. Ignore anyone who approaches you indoors and go straight to the official taxi stands outside. Avoiding overcharges and filing a complaint

Most rides are uneventful, but Seoul city officials have acknowledged a steady stream of complaints from tourists, typically about meters not being switched on, unexplained surcharges or detours on airport runs. A few habits protect you:

  • Make sure the meter starts when you set off, or book through an app so the trip is tracked.
  • Note the full license plate number, or take a quick photo of the plate or the driver ID card on the dashboard.
  • Check that a night or out-of-area surcharge on the receipt matches the time and route you actually took.
  • Keep the receipt, your card statement and any app booking details until you get home.

If something goes wrong, you can report it to Seoul's 120 Dasan Call Center or scan the QR code on the complaint stickers inside Seoul taxis, which opens a short online form. For help in English, Japanese or Chinese during your trip, the Korea Tourism Organization's 1330 Korea Travel Helpline can interpret by phone or chat.

Lost something in a cab? This is where an app booking or card receipt pays off: both identify the exact car, which makes it far easier for the operator to track down your item.

Taxis outside Seoul

The same basics apply across the country: red vacancy lights, meters, cards accepted and app booking through Kakao T in most cities. Base fares and surcharge rules are set by each city or province, so the numbers above are only a guide outside the capital. FAQ

Can I pay for a Korean taxi with a foreign credit card?

Yes, street taxis in Seoul generally accept credit cards, cash and transit cards such as T-money. App bookings are paid in the app if you set up automatic payment.

How much is a taxi in Seoul?

As of October 2026, a regular Seoul taxi starts at about 4,800 won for the first 1.6 km during the day, rising to about 5,800 to 6,700 won late at night. Deluxe black taxis start at about 7,000 won.

Which taxi app should a tourist use in Korea?

Kakao T is the most widely used app in Korea. Kakao Mobility's K.ride and the regular Uber app are designed to be easier for overseas visitors to sign up for and pay with foreign cards, though K.ride adds a service fee.

Do I need to tip taxi drivers in Korea?

No. Tipping is not expected; you pay the metered fare plus any tolls.

Is there a surcharge for a taxi from Incheon Airport to Seoul?

Seoul taxis between Seoul and Incheon Airport do not charge the out-of-area surcharge, but expressway tolls and any late-night surcharge still apply.

What should I do if I think I was overcharged?

Keep your receipt and note the license plate, then report it to Seoul's 120 Dasan Call Center or scan the complaint QR code inside the taxi.
