How to Order Food Delivery in Korea as a Visitor

Korea's delivery apps now work far better for tourists, with English menus and overseas payment options. This guide covers which app to use, how to sign up and pay, and how to give an address riders can find.

By coverfo · Last checked October 31, 2026 · 7 min read

Food delivery is part of everyday life in Korea. Fried chicken to your hotel room at midnight, a full Korean set meal after a long day of walking, or a picnic delivered to a riverside park are all normal requests here. For a long time the apps were hard for visitors to use, because they expected a Korean phone number and a Korean card. That has changed a lot in 2025 and 2026. This guide explains which app to use, how to sign up and pay as a tourist, how to give an address that riders can actually find, and what to expect when the food arrives.

The three main delivery apps

Korea has three big delivery platforms. All of them list thousands of restaurants in Seoul, Busan and other cities, and the menus and prices are set by each restaurant.

For a first-time visitor, Baemin is currently the most tourist-friendly choice. Its operator, Woowa Brothers, added English, Chinese and Japanese to the ordering flow in early 2026, covering search, restaurant and menu selection, payment and live delivery tracking. Translations are machine-generated, so menu names can be a bit odd, but the photos and prices make it easy to follow. Coupang Eats is a good second option if you already use Coupang or if Baemin does not cover your area well.

Install your chosen app and try the sign-up steps before you are hungry. If anything fails, you still have time to try another app or ask your hotel for help.

Signing up and paying without a Korean phone number

The hardest part used to be verification. Korean apps normally confirm your identity through a local mobile number. As of 2026, Baemin offers two routes:

  • Full account: for people with a Korean phone number and a foreigner registration number (residents).
  • Order without joining: visitors can place an order by verifying an overseas phone number with a text message code. This was described by Baemin and the Seoul city government in August 2026.

To receive that code, your home SIM needs to be able to get text messages in Korea. If you rely only on a data eSIM, check whether it can receive SMS, or keep your home line active for messages. Our Korea travel checklist covers SIM and eSIM choices in more detail.

For payment, foreign-issued Visa, Mastercard, JCB and American Express cards can be used through Apple Pay on Baemin, and overseas credit cards, WeChat Pay and Alipay+ are also supported. Coupang Eats allows you to register an overseas card. Card acceptance can still fail for individual cards because of your bank's fraud checks, so it is wise to have a second card ready.

How to order, step by step

  1. Set the language. Open the app and switch to English if it does not ask you automatically. Baemin shows a language pop-up on first launch.
  2. Enter your delivery address. This is the step that matters most; see the next section.
  3. Browse. Restaurants are grouped by category, such as chicken, pizza, Korean, Chinese, snacks and late-night food. Each listing shows the estimated delivery time and the delivery fee.
  4. Check the minimum order. Many restaurants set a minimum order amount, so a single small item may not be accepted on its own. The app shows this before checkout.
  5. Add a request note. Use the request field to tell the rider where to go, for example the hotel lobby or a room number, or to ask for no cutlery.
  6. Pay and track. After payment, the app shows when the restaurant accepts the order and where the rider is.

Delivery fees are set partly by the restaurant and vary with distance and demand. The final total, including any delivery tip, is shown on the payment screen before you confirm, so check it there rather than relying on the price on the menu page.

Popular things to order

  • Fried chicken, often half crispy and half in a sweet spicy sauce, usually with pickled radish on the side.
  • Jokbal (braised pork trotters) and bossam (boiled pork with kimchi and lettuce wraps), classic late-night sharing dishes.
  • Tteokbokki (spicy rice cakes) with fried snacks and gimbap.
  • Korean-style Chinese food, such as jjajangmyeon noodles in black bean sauce and sweet and sour pork.

Giving an address the rider can find

Most delivery problems come from the address. A rider who cannot find you will usually call, and a call in fast Korean is hard to handle. A few habits help a lot:

  • Get the Korean address from your hotel. Ask the front desk for the building's road-name address in Korean, and copy it into the app instead of typing an English version.
  • Add the details. Include the building name, the floor and your room number. For apartments and guesthouses, add the building or block number.
  • Ask about hotel rules. Some hotels let riders come up to rooms, while others ask guests to meet riders in the lobby. Find out before ordering and write the answer in the request note.
  • Door codes. If your rental has a building entrance code and the host allows it, put it in the request note so the rider can get in.
Write your request note in simple English plus the hotel's Korean name. A short line such as "Lobby, Room 1203" is easier for a rider to read than a long explanation.

Many orders in Korea are left at the door rather than handed over, especially at homes and rentals. If you would rather meet the rider, say so in the note.

Picnics by the Han River

Ordering chicken to a Han River park is a well-known Seoul pastime. Because parks do not have street addresses, Seoul runs designated delivery zones where riders bring orders and customers pick them up. In September 2026 the city expanded these to 17 spots across 11 Han River parks, including Yeouido, Ttukseom, Banpo, Mangwon, Jamsil and Nanji. Find the zone nearest to you (they are marked with signs), use it as your delivery point, mention it in the request note, and walk over when the app shows the rider is close.

Han River parks are an easy addition to a city day. Our 3-day Seoul itinerary includes an evening by the river where a delivered dinner fits in well.

Etiquette and practical tips

  • Tipping is not expected. Any delivery tip is part of the fee shown in the app, and you do not hand riders extra cash.
  • Peak times are slower. Weekend evenings, rainy days and big sports nights can stretch delivery times. Order a little earlier if you are on a schedule.
  • Allergies and diets. Machine translation may not catch every ingredient. If you have a serious allergy, choose simple dishes and use a translation app to ask the restaurant through the app's chat or request note.
  • Rubbish. Korea sorts waste carefully. In hotels, bag your leftovers and containers neatly; in rentals, follow the host's instructions for food waste and recycling.
  • Help in English. The Korea Travel Helpline 1330 can help with general travel problems in several languages.

FAQ

Can tourists use Korean food delivery apps?

Yes. As of 2026, Baemin offers English, Chinese and Japanese and lets visitors order by verifying an overseas phone number by text message, and Coupang Eats offers English and accepts registered overseas cards.

Do I need a Korean phone number to order delivery?

Not on Baemin, which allows ordering without an account after SMS verification of an overseas number. Make sure your phone can receive text messages in Korea.

Can I pay with a foreign credit card?

Baemin accepts overseas credit cards, WeChat Pay and Alipay+, and since June 2026 Apple Pay with foreign-issued cards. Coupang Eats lets you register an overseas card.

Should I tip the delivery rider?

No. Tipping is not customary in Korea; any delivery fee or tip is shown and charged in the app.

Can I get food delivered to a Han River park?

Yes. Seoul has designated delivery zones in Han River parks, expanded in September 2026 to 17 spots across 11 parks, where riders hand over orders.
