Jeju Island in 3 Days: A Road Trip Itinerary with Flight and Car Rental Tips

Three days is enough to see Jeju's volcanic coast if you have a car and split the island into east, south and west. This plan covers the route, where to sleep, and the documents foreign drivers need to rent a car.

By coverfo · Last checked October 31, 2026 · 8 min read

Jeju is a volcanic island off Korea's south coast, and it rewards anyone with a car. Buses do reach the main sights, but the best parts of the island sit between them: coastal roads, small harbors, tangerine farms and grassy volcanic cones called oreum. This three-day plan splits the island into east, south and west so you never drive more than you need to, and it ends close to the airport for an easy flight out. It assumes you arrive at Jeju International Airport (CJU) in the morning and leave on the evening of day three.

Getting to Jeju and planning the loop

Most visitors fly in from Seoul. The Gimpo (GMP) to Jeju route is one of the busiest air corridors in the world, with flights roughly every few minutes at peak times and a flight time of about an hour. Several Korean carriers fly it, so it is worth comparing a few departure times rather than booking the first fare you see. There are also flights from Busan, other Korean cities and a number of international airports in Asia.

Jeju is roughly oval, with Jeju City and the airport on the north coast, Seogwipo on the south coast and Hallasan, Korea's highest mountain, in the middle. Driving around the island in one go takes most of a day, so the plan below treats it as three arcs and books a different base each night.

Renting a car in Jeju as a foreigner

The paperwork is the part people get wrong. To rent a car in Korea on a foreign licence you generally need three things: your passport, your valid home-country driver's licence, and an International Driving Permit (IDP) issued under the Geneva or Vienna road traffic conventions. Korea's official culture and information service notes that an IDP is accepted for one year from your date of entry. Other documents sold as an "international driver's licence" are not the same thing and rental desks turn them away. Get the IDP from the official issuing body in your home country before you travel, and carry it together with your original licence.

  • Age and experience: major Korean rental companies typically require drivers to be at least 21 and to have held a licence for at least a year. Higher age limits often apply to larger or premium cars.
  • Payment: many companies accept only a credit card, and the name should match the booking and the driver.
  • Insurance: basic cover is included, but there is usually a deductible. Paying for the higher-level damage waiver is common in Jeju, and you should read exactly what it excludes (tyres, wheels and the "non-operation" fee charged while a car is repaired are typical extras).
  • Car sharing: app-based car sharing in Korea usually requires a Korean licence, so visitors should plan on a regular rental.

At Jeju Airport, rental companies do not hand over cars at the terminal. Follow the signs for rental cars to the covered walkway and the rental car shuttle area across the road from the arrivals hall, then board the shuttle marked with your company's name. Their lots are a short ride away. Allow at least an hour from landing to driving off, longer on weekends and holidays.

On the road, Jeju has no toll highways, but it does have plenty of speed cameras, and speed limits drop sharply near villages and schools. For navigation, Naver Map or Kakao Map work far better in Korea than most foreign map apps; see our guide to Korea's essential travel apps. Day 1: the east coast and Seongsan

From the airport, head east along the north coast. Hamdeok Beach, about half an hour from the airport, is a good first stop for pale sand, shallow turquoise water and a short walk up the small hill at its eastern end.

Continue inland to Manjanggul, one of the lava tubes that make up Jeju's UNESCO World Natural Heritage site. The cave was closed for more than two years after a rockfall and reopened at the end of May 2026. Only part of the tube is open to visitors, along a walk of about a kilometre in the cool, damp dark, so bring a light jacket even in summer.

In the afternoon, drive to Seongsan Ilchulbong, the tuff cone that rises straight out of the sea on the island's eastern tip. The climb to the crater rim is on stairs and takes most people 20 to 30 minutes each way. Finish the day a few minutes south at Seopjikoji, a windswept headland with a lighthouse and wide sea views, and if you like immersive art, the nearby Bunker de Lumières light exhibition fills an old communications bunker. Sleep in Seongsan so you can catch sunrise the next morning without a long drive.

Day 2: Udo and the south coast

Early risers can climb Seongsan Ilchulbong again for sunrise, which is how it got its name, "sunrise peak". Opening times shift with the seasons, so check before you set an alarm.

If you have a full morning, take the ferry from Seongsan Port to Udo, a small island with white-sand beaches and stone walls. The crossing takes about 10 to 15 minutes. Taking a rental car onto the Udo ferry is restricted, so most visitors park at the port and get around Udo by bus, bicycle or a small rental scooter. Check the current vehicle rules at the ferry terminal before you go. Check the last ferry back before you start exploring.

After lunch, drive south-west to Seogwipo, roughly an hour away. Jeongbang Falls drops almost directly onto the shore, and Cheonjiyeon Falls sits at the end of a gentle, leafy walk that is also pleasant in the evening. Between the two, wander Seogwipo Maeil Olle Market for tangerines, grilled fish and snacks. In late afternoon, continue to Jungmun to see the hexagonal basalt columns of the Jusangjeolli cliffs, where waves break against rock that looks almost man-made.

Hiking Hallasan instead? The two summit trails, Seongpanak and Gwaneumsa, require a free advance reservation through the national park's official booking site, and slots on weekends go quickly. The Eorimok and Yeongsil trails, which reach the Witse-oreum area below the summit, do not need a reservation. From 1 October 2026 to 31 March 2027, winter hours apply: trails open at 5 a.m., and the last entry for Seongpanak and Gwaneumsa is 11:30 a.m.

Day 3: the west coast and back to the airport

Head west from Jungmun to the area around Sanbangsan, a dome-shaped lava mountain, and the coastal path at Songaksan, where the trail looks out to two small islands and, on clear days, Hallasan behind you. This corner of the island is quieter in the morning.

Drive north through the green tea fields near the O'sulloc Tea Museum, a popular stop for matcha desserts and views over neat rows of tea bushes. Continue to Hyeopjae Beach on the north-west coast, known for its clear shallow water and the view of Biyangdo island offshore.

On the way back toward the airport, take the coastal road through Aewol, lined with cafés facing the sea, for a final coffee. Aim to reach your rental lot with plenty of time to refuel, return the car, and ride the shuttle back to the terminal. Domestic check-in at Jeju is efficient, but holiday weekends get crowded.

Fill the tank near Jeju City rather than at the last station by the airport, and keep the fuel receipt in case the lot asks for proof of a full tank.

Practical tips for driving and eating in Jeju

  • Weather: Jeju is windy and changes fast, and the east and south can look very different on the same afternoon. Check the forecast each morning and swap days if one side of the island is clear.
  • Parking: most beaches, falls and parks have their own lots. Village streets are narrow, so park at the edge and walk in.
  • Food: look for black pork barbecue, seafood noodle soup, abalone porridge and anything made with Jeju tangerines. Many small restaurants close between lunch and dinner and stop seating early in the evening.
  • Payment: cards are widely accepted, but carry some cash for market stalls and small ferry or parking fees.
  • Admission fees: the big natural sites charge modest entry fees and often close one day a month, so check before you drive out of your way.

FAQ

Can I rent a car in Jeju with only my home-country licence?

Generally no. Rental companies ask for your passport, your valid home licence and an International Driving Permit issued under the Geneva or Vienna convention, which is accepted for one year from entry.

Is three days enough for Jeju?

Three days is enough for the coastal highlights if you drive and split the island into east, south and west. Add a fourth day if you want to hike to the summit of Hallasan.

Do I need a reservation to hike Hallasan?

The Seongpanak and Gwaneumsa summit trails require a free advance reservation through the national park's booking system. The Eorimok and Yeongsil trails do not.

Can I take my rental car to Udo?

Usually not. Rental cars are restricted on the Udo ferry apart from limited exceptions, so most visitors park at Seongsan Port and use buses, bikes or scooters on Udo.

How long is the flight from Seoul to Jeju?

About an hour from Gimpo Airport, with frequent departures through the day.
