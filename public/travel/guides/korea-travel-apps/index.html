Korea's Essential Travel Apps: What to Install Before You Go

Many everyday tasks in Korea, from getting directions to calling a taxi, work best through local apps rather than the ones you use at home. Here are the apps worth setting up before your trip, what they do, and the catches to know about.

By coverfo · Last checked October 31, 2026 · 7 min read

Korea runs on smartphone apps, and many of them were built for people with a Korean phone number, a Korean bank card and fluent Korean. That has been changing quickly, with several big local apps adding English menus and accepting foreign cards over the past two years. Still, the apps that work best for a visitor are not always the ones you use at home. This guide walks through the handful worth installing before your flight, what each one is good for, and where the usual catches are. Details are as of October 2026; app policies change often, so check the store listing before you rely on a feature.

Maps and directions: Naver Map first

For years, Google Maps in Korea has been limited because of government rules on exporting detailed map data. It shows places and public transit routes, but not turn-by-turn walking or driving navigation, and walking sections appear only as rough dotted lines. In February 2026 the government gave Google conditional approval to export high-precision map data, and Google has said it plans to bring full navigation to Korea. As of the latest reports we found, though, no launch date had been announced, so do not count on Google Maps alone.

Naver Map is the app most Koreans use, and it is the one we recommend as your main map. It has an English interface, proper walking directions and detailed public transit routes, which is exactly what Google Maps is missing. Since late 2024, Naver Map has also translated restaurant reviews and search filters into English, Japanese and Chinese, so you can browse menus and opinions without guessing. Kakao Map is the main alternative and is worth having as a backup.

Search for places in Korean when English results come up empty. Copying the Korean name from a hotel website or a sign into the search bar is often the quickest way to find the exact spot.

Taxis: Kakao T, K.ride or Uber

Booking a cab by app saves you from explaining an address in Korean, and the route and fare are tracked. Kakao T is by far the biggest local taxi app, and most drivers are on it. K.ride, also from Kakao Mobility, was built for international visitors: you can sign up with Google, Apple or email, pay with overseas cards or wallets such as Alipay+, and message drivers with automatic translation. It adds a 10% service fee on Korean taxi categories. The regular Uber app also works in Korea and connects you with licensed taxis.

Fares, night surcharges and airport tips are covered in detail in our guide to taking a taxi in Korea.

Trains: KORAIL+

Korea's national rail operator, Korail, runs the KTX high-speed trains and most regular lines. Its official app is now called KORAIL+ (it was previously known as Korail Talk). The app is available in English and several other languages, including Japanese, Chinese, Thai and Vietnamese, and lets you search trains, book seats, store your ticket on your phone and buy the KORAIL Pass. Book early for Friday evenings, weekends and holidays, when the Seoul to Busan route in particular fills up. Our Seoul to Busan by KTX guide explains the routes, stations and how SRT trains fit in.

Translation: Papago

Papago, made by Naver, is a popular translator for Korean and a good first choice. It handles typed text, speech and photos, so you can point your camera at a menu, a sign or a pharmacy label and get a translation on screen. A conversation mode lets you and another person speak in turn in your own languages, and text translation also works offline, which helps when your connection drops in the subway. Papago supports around 14 languages, covering most of the major ones visitors speak.

Machine translation is good for everyday situations but can still miss nuance. For anything serious, such as describing symptoms or a food allergy, keep sentences short and simple, and show the translated text rather than relying on the app's voice.

Prepare a few translated phrases before you need them: your hotel address, any allergies, and "please take me here". Save them as screenshots so they work without data.

Food delivery: Baemin and friends

Ordering food to your room is a big part of daily life in Korea, from fried chicken at midnight to full Korean breakfasts. The three main apps are Baemin, Coupang Eats and Yogiyo. Baemin added English, Chinese and Japanese menus in 2026, and from June 2026 it began accepting Apple Pay with overseas-issued Visa, Mastercard, JCB and American Express cards. That makes it the most visitor-friendly option at the moment. Sign-up and payment rules for the other apps change regularly, so try setting up an account before you arrive.

A clear delivery address is the trickiest part. Copy your hotel's Korean address exactly, add your room number, and leave a short note for the rider. Our guide to ordering food delivery in Korea covers this step by step.

Paying for transit and getting help

Seoul's subways and buses are paid with a rechargeable transit card. The best known is T-money, which is widely sold as a physical card at convenience stores and subway stations; topping it up with cash is the simplest method. Seoul taxis accept it too. Mobile T-money options also exist for phones, but which ones accept foreign-issued cards has been changing, so check the official T-money app before you rely on it. If that does not work with your card, a physical card topped up with cash is the reliable fallback.

Finally, save the 1330 Korea Travel Helpline, run by the Korea Tourism Organization. You can call it or use its web chat for interpretation and travel information in English, Japanese and Chinese. It is genuinely useful when you are stuck at a ticket machine or need to explain something to a shop or taxi driver. Calls over Wi-Fi are free, though mobile data charges may apply.

A final word on setup. Several of these apps send a text message code when you register or add a card, so do the sign-ups at home while your regular number still works, rather than on airport Wi-Fi after landing. Set each app's language to English in its settings if it does not switch automatically, add your payment card, and open each one once to accept permissions for location and notifications. It also helps to save your hotel as a favorite in your map app and taxi app, so getting back at the end of a long day takes two taps instead of a struggle with Korean addresses. Keep your phone charged too: with maps, translation and tickets all on one device, a small power bank is worth packing.

FAQ

Does Google Maps work in Korea?

Partly. It shows places and public transit routes, but as of the latest reports it still lacks full walking and driving navigation, so Naver Map or Kakao Map is more reliable for getting around.

Is Naver Map available in English?

Yes. Naver Map has an English interface, and since late 2024 it also translates restaurant reviews and search filters into English, Japanese and Chinese.

What is the best translation app for Korea?

Papago, made by Naver, is a popular choice for Korean. It translates text, speech and photos, and can translate text offline.

Can tourists use Korean food delivery apps?

Yes, increasingly. Baemin added English menus in 2026 and began accepting Apple Pay with overseas-issued cards in June 2026, though setup can still take some effort.

What is the official Korail app called?

It is KORAIL+, previously called Korail Talk. It is available in English and lets you search and book trains and buy the KORAIL Pass.
