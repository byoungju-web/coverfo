Seoul to Busan by KTX: How to Book and Ride in 2026

Since September 2026 the former SRT trains run as part of the KTX network, so every Seoul to Busan high-speed train can be booked in one place. Here is how to pick a station, buy a ticket and board without stress.

By coverfo · Last checked October 31, 2026 · 8 min read

The trip from Seoul to Busan is the most popular intercity journey in Korea, and the high-speed train is usually the easiest way to make it. You skip airport security, you leave from the middle of the city, and you arrive at Busan Station close to the old port, the markets and the metro. This guide explains how the system works after the big changes of 2026, where to buy tickets, what to expect on board, and a few habits that save stress on the day.

What changed in 2026: KTX and SRT are now one network

Until recently Korea had two separate high-speed train brands. KTX, run by the national operator Korail, left mainly from Seoul Station. SRT, run by a separate company called SR, left from Suseo Station in southeastern Seoul. They had different apps, different memberships and slightly different prices, which confused many visitors.

That split has ended. Korea's competition regulator approved Korail taking over SR in early August 2026, and from September 1, 2026 the former SRT services run as part of the KTX network. According to the Ministry of Land, Infrastructure and Transport, the main effects for travelers are:

  • One place to book. Trains from both Seoul Station and Suseo can be searched and booked together in Korail's app, now called Korail+ (it replaced the old Korail Talk app in August 2026), and on Korail's website.
  • Lower fares. KTX fares were brought down to the former SRT level, an average cut of about 10%.
  • More seats. The government reported roughly 15,000 extra seats per day on weekdays and up to about 17,000 on weekends, with around 30% more seats on trains from Suseo.
  • A longer booking window. From October 1, 2026, rail tickets can be bought up to two months before departure instead of one.

The physical trains will look mixed for a while. Officials have said former SRT trains will be gradually repainted into KTX livery over several years, so do not be surprised if your "KTX" ticket from Suseo puts you on a train that still says SRT on the side.

If an older blog post tells you to download a separate SRT app for Suseo trains, it is out of date. As of October 2026, integrated trains are sold through Korail+ and Korail's website.

Which Seoul station should you use?

Both main departure points now sell tickets through the same system, so the best choice is simply the station closer to where you are staying.

StationAreaGood for
Seoul StationCentral Seoul, near Namdaemun and Myeong-dongMost visitors; also linked to the airport railroad from Incheon
Suseo StationSoutheast Seoul, south of the riverTravelers staying in Gangnam, Jamsil or southeast Seoul

If you are staying north of the river around Myeong-dong, Insadong or Hongdae, Seoul Station is usually the simplest. If your hotel is in Gangnam, crossing the city to Seoul Station can easily take longer than the ride from Suseo. Our Seoul neighborhood guide can help if you have not booked a base yet.

At the Busan end, most trains finish at Busan Station, which connects to Busan Metro Line 1. In September 2026 Korail also added some new routings via other stations, such as Gupo in western Busan, so check the arrival station on your ticket before you get off.

Travel time and fares

The Seoul to Busan line is about 447 km long. Depending on how many stops a train makes, the ride usually takes roughly two and a half to three hours. Faster services with fewer stops sit at the shorter end; trains stopping at more intermediate cities take longer.

Fares vary by train, seat class and discount, so treat these numbers as a guide and confirm the price shown at checkout. First class (called teuksil in Korean) costs more and offers wider seats. Standard class (ilbansil) is comfortable enough for a trip of under three hours.

How to book your ticket

Official VisitKorea information lists several channels: station ticket offices and kiosks, Korail's website, the Korail+ app, and train booking inside NAVER and Kakao T. For most foreign visitors, the realistic choices are these:

  1. Korail's website in English. Korail runs a foreign-language booking site that supports seven languages, including English, Chinese, Japanese, Thai and Vietnamese, as of October 2026. The government has said French, German and Mongolian will be added. Overseas credit cards are accepted; WeChat Pay and Alipay were described as still in development.
  2. The Korail+ app. This is the same app Koreans use. It shows Seoul Station and Suseo trains together, lets you choose a seat, and stores your ticket on your phone. Using it with all its benefits requires Korail's integrated membership.
  3. At the station. Ticket counters and kiosks work fine on quiet days, but popular times can sell out, so do not count on buying at the last minute.

Book early for Friday evenings, Sunday afternoons and the major holidays, Seollal (Lunar New Year) and Chuseok, when seats disappear quickly. Outside those times, booking a few days ahead is often enough, although the two-month window means you can lock in a seat as soon as your flights are confirmed.

Take a screenshot of your ticket with the train number, car number and seat number. KTX stations have no ticket gates, but you will need those details to find your seat, and phone data can be slow in crowded concourses.

What about the Korail Pass?

The Korail Pass is sold only to international travelers and gives unlimited standard-class rides for 2, 3, 4 or 5 days within a ten-day period, with up to two seat reservations per day. It can pay off if you plan several long train trips in a short time; for a single Seoul to Busan return it usually does not. The VisitKorea page, last updated in August 2026, lists SRT trains as not covered. Since the integration, check with Korail whether trains departing from Suseo are now included before you rely on the pass for them.

On the day: boarding and on board

  • Arrive 15 to 20 minutes early. Big stations are busy, and finding the right platform plus your car's boarding spot takes a few minutes.
  • Check the departure board. Boards show the train number, destination and platform in Korean and English. Platforms usually appear on the board shortly before departure.
  • Find your car number. Platform floor markings and signs show where each car stops. Board at your car's door rather than walking through the train with luggage.
  • Luggage. There are racks at the end of each car and overhead shelves for smaller bags. Large suitcases go in the end racks, so board promptly if you are carrying one.
  • Facilities. Trains have restrooms and vending machines, and most offer free Wi-Fi, though the connection can be patchy.
  • Quiet carriages by habit. Koreans generally keep phone calls short and quiet on trains. Step into the vestibule between cars if you need to talk.

If no seats are left, standing or unreserved tickets are sometimes sold on busy routes. They are fine for a short hop but tiring for the full trip to Busan.

Arriving in Busan

Busan Station is right next to the Chinatown area and a short ride from Nampo-dong and Jagalchi Market. From the station, Busan Metro Line 1 runs through the city center; for Haeundae Beach you will need to transfer to Line 2, which takes a while, so many visitors with luggage simply take a taxi. Our taxi guide explains how to hail or call one with Kakao T, and the travel apps guide covers the map and translation apps that make the first hour in a new city easier.

If you need help in English during your trip, the Korea Travel Helpline 1330 offers multilingual support, and VisitKorea's train page lists an English-language Korail customer center at 1599-7777.

Planning a return the same week? Book both directions at once. The return leg on Sunday afternoon is often the first to sell out.

FAQ

Do I still need the SRT app to travel from Suseo Station?

No. Since September 1, 2026 the former SRT trains run as part of KTX, and integrated trains are sold through Korail's Korail+ app and Korail's website.

How long does the KTX take from Seoul to Busan?

Roughly two and a half to three hours, depending on how many stops the train makes.

How much is a KTX ticket from Seoul to Busan?

A standard seat from Seoul Station to Busan was about 54,400 won as of October 2026, after an average 10% fare cut in September 2026. Prices vary by train and discount, so check the checkout screen.

How far in advance can I book?

From October 1, 2026, rail tickets can be booked up to two months before departure. Book early for weekends and the Seollal and Chuseok holidays.

Can foreigners pay with an overseas credit card?

Yes. Korail's foreign-language booking site accepts overseas credit cards; WeChat Pay and Alipay were still being developed as of October 2026.

Is the Korail Pass worth it for Seoul to Busan?

Usually not for a single return trip. It makes more sense if you take several long train rides within a few days, and you should confirm with Korail which trains it covers since the 2026 integration.
